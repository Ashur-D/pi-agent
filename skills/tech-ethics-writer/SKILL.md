# Technology & Society Academic Essay Writing

Framework for writing rigorous, critical essays on technology, sociology, ethics, and human disruption.

## 1. Core Thematic Lenses

When analyzing technological disruption, consider:
- **Technological Determinism vs. Social Constructivism (SCOT):** Does technology shape society, or do human social/economic needs shape technological trajectory?
- **Surveillance Capitalism & Privacy:** Data extraction as an economic model, predictive behavior modification (Zuboff).
- **Algorithmic Bias & Power:** Inherent biases in training data, reinforcement of systemic discrimination, the "black box" accountability problem.
- **Labor Automation & Economic Stratification:** Shift in labor value, deskilling, platform economy (gig work), digital divide.
- **Transhumanism & Digital Identity:** Blurring lines between human agency and synthetic augmentation, cognitive impacts of continuous connectivity.

## 2. Argument Construction & Essay Workflow

1. **Working Thesis:** Must take a defensible, non-obvious stance (avoid: "Technology has both good and bad sides"). State the specific mechanism of impact.
   * *Weak:* "AI affects artists negatively."
   * *Strong:* "Generative AI systems commodify artistic style without consent, dismantling traditional intellectual property protections and concentrating cultural output in proprietary platforms."
2. **Counter-Argumentation:** Devote substantive space to the strongest opposing argument, not a strawman. Directly refute it with empirical or historical precedents.
3. **Evidence Integration:** Synthesize sociologists, philosophers, and real-world case studies rather than stating unsupported opinions.
4. **Conclusion:** Never repeat the introduction verbatim. State the broader implication: what must policy, engineering, or society do next?

## 3. Style and Academic Honesty

- Run drafts through the `unslop` skill to ensure original, non-generic prose.
- Adhere strictly to APA 7th or MLA 9th citation styles for all references and in-text quotes.
